Ten tasks: simulation vs real robot

NeurIPS 2026 RoboSynChallenge: Overall Champion, Simulation Track (Team Lead)

Team fmc3-no1. The challenge ran ten bimanual manipulation tasks on the AgileX CobotMagic (two PiPER arms, three RGB cameras): a simulation round in EmbodiChain first, then real robots. For each task, the left clip is the π0.5 we submitted, succeeding in the official evaluation; the right clip is the world action model Phoenix-MWA succeeding on its own on our real-robot bench.

Our methods ↓
  • Both clips share one layout: the top camera on the left, the left and right wrist cameras stacked on the right.
  • The simulation evaluation randomizes object poses, lighting and textures, so the simulated scene changes color every second.
  • Videos load only when you press play, and only one plays at a time.

Methods

Summarized from the technical report we submitted

The simulation policy, with human takeovers, collected the first real-robot data; that data post-trained the world action model, whose own runs and takeovers fed the next round.

Simulation: a data-driven π0.5 recipe

  • One π0.5 (openpi, JAX) fine-tuned per task, predicting 50-step delta joint actions with EMA 0.99.
  • Re-ran the official scripted experts with new seeds and kept only episodes the official judge passed, doubling the expert data to about 20k episodes.
  • Replayed the experts with other settings (for example a higher handover point) on scenes where the policy failed, then fine-tuned with a small learning rate.
  • Wider randomization: object pose ranges ×1.6, initial joint noise ×2, textures from 100 to 300, a moving top camera; the last round mixed original and wide data 1:2.
  • Chose per task how many of the 50 predicted steps to execute before replanning; this mattered more than most training-side changes.
  • For the millimeter-tight sample_loading, rebuilt the scripted expert so it seats the tube and retries.

Real robot: a world action model trained on real data

  • A digital twin traced the sim-to-real failure to the wrist-camera pose, so we kept the organizers' camera layout on our bench and fixed the model with real data.
  • After comparing π0.5 with three world action models, chose Phoenix-MWA (a Wan2.2-5B video expert coupled with a ~1B-parameter action expert, 32 steps per call) and post-trained it on real data only on 32 A100s.
  • A web console runs the policy; operators take over with leader arms, and every takeover becomes training data.
  • Cut the failed lead-in before each takeover, collapsed pauses over 0.5 s to one frame and dropped static tails: the main fix for stalls.
  • Observation-only rules live in the policy adapter: per-task gripper clamp thresholds, masking the top camera and nudging forward when stuck, joint limits and per-step caps.
  • Action-chunk inpainting: an RTC soft mask conditions each new chunk on the unexecuted tail of the previous one. It changes inference only, needs no retraining, and smooths the seams.

We also tried Spatial Forcing, RECAP in simulation, RLT, RECAP-ACP on the real robot and HIL-SERL; none paid off within the competition timeline.
